- **★★★★★ THE KERNEL-SIDE FIX IS WRITTEN — and the gap is exactly where predicted** (kernel `5d8645f6`, `--scope core` building). The kernel *already* protects against this: `_pmap_cacheOpBeforeChange()` flushes when a mapping goes cached→non-cached/unmapped. But its **only two callers are `_pmap_enter()` and the unmap path** — **`_pmap_destroy()` releases a whole process's pages with no cache maintenance at all.** So on process exit, dirty lines survive into the free pool, and nothing retires them before the pages are handed to the next owner.
  - **Fix placed where the guarantee is needed:** `_pmap_cacheOpAfterChange()` now clean+invalidates when the *new* mapping is `MAIR_IDX_NONCACHED` — the data-cache counterpart of the instruction-cache handling already there. Restricted to Normal-NC (real RAM mapped uncached); Device mappings hold no lines and are untouched. Cost is one page flush per uncached mapping — rare (DMA buffers) — versus flushing every page at teardown.
  - **Blast radius is the point:** this covers **every uncached DMA mapping in the system** — SD, genet, USB, WiFi — not just V3D. The V3D `dc civac` remains as the local, already-verified repair; this is the general one.
